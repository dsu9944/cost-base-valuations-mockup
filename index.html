Why it matters

Tax changes make certain dates split your capital gain

Recent tax settings mean certain dates split your capital gain — especially 30 June 2027 for many investors — so a signed market value as at that date can matter even if you’re not selling yet. The same idea applies when a home first becomes an investment, or when cost base / estate work needs a clear dated figure.

Why the tax change makes the date valuable

A free straight-line approach assumes the property grew smoothly to the split date. Markets don’t. Getting a signed market value closer to 30 June 2027 (or your first-rented / other tax date) locks how much gain sits before vs after that date — while comps and condition are still current — instead of reconstructing years later.

FAQ

Common questions

Straight answers in plain English. Not tax advice.

I’m not selling — why now?

Because the tax date that splits a later gain can arrive before any sale. For many investors, 30 June 2027 is that date. Locking a signed market value closer to the day — while comps and condition are still current — is often clearer than reconstructing years later with a straight-line formula. Your accountant applies any report to your own facts.

Isn’t the ATO formula free?

Yes — and free can still mis-split the gain. Property doesn’t grow in a straight line. Neighbourhoods boom, stall, and jump. A signed market value as at the tax date (for example 30 June 2027) is the comparison against that formula assumption. We don’t sell online estimates.

I can’t find the old contract — am I stuck?

No. Start with records if you can. Gaps are common. If paperwork stays incomplete, a signed retrospective as at the relevant tax date can still give your accountant a usable number. No shame either way.

What date matters if I moved out and rented?

Often the first-rented / first income-producing date. A signed retrospective market value as at that day is what many accountants ask for — framed as market value as at the date, not a live listing appraisal.
